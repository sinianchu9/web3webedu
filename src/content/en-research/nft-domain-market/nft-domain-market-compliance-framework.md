## Abstract

NFT domain trading platforms face unique compliance challenges under current regulatory frameworks. As an emerging digital asset class, NFT domain transactions typically occur on decentralized marketplaces like OpenSea, introducing complexities that combine traditional domain management (ICANN) with blockchain-native mechanisms. Under current regulatory frameworks, NFT domain trading platforms may face compliance requirements related to user identification, anti-money laundering (AML), and Know Your Customer (KYC), which create potential friction with blockchain technology's anonymity and global nature.

The core conclusion is that NFT domain trading platforms should build a multi-layered compliance framework that combines platform-level user agreements and market monitoring with the digital asset registration mechanisms of underlying blockchains. Digital asset registration, particularly on-chain ownership records achieved through the Ethereum Name Service (ENS), forms the trust foundation of the NFT domain ecosystem, though its legal effect and cross-border enforcement require further clarification and development.

## Problem Definition

The core question this article examines is: under current regulatory frameworks, how can NFT domain trading platforms balance the tension between decentralized technology and compliance requirements, and what role do digital asset registration mechanisms play in the compliance framework.

## Background

NFT domains, such as those registered through the Ethereum Name Service (ENS), have become important digital identities and assets in the Web3 ecosystem. They not only provide human-readable blockchain addresses but also exist as tradable non-fungible tokens (NFTs), with ownership recorded on public blockchains (ENS Docs, 2023). Leading NFT trading platforms like OpenSea provide active markets for these digital assets, facilitating their rapid development (OpenSea, 2023).

Unlike traditional domain systems regulated by the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN) (ICANN, 2023), NFT domains operate in a decentralized manner, presenting unique compliance challenges in terms of ownership verification, dispute resolution, and regulatory applicability.

## Core Conclusions

1. **Necessity of Multi-layered Compliance Framework**: NFT domain trading platforms should establish multi-layered compliance frameworks combining platform-level user agreements, market monitoring, and on-chain digital asset registration mechanisms.

2. **Dual Role of Digital Asset Registration**: On-chain registration provided by ENS and similar services offers technical guarantees for ownership, but requires deeper integration with existing legal systems to ensure legal effect.

3. **Balancing Privacy and Compliance**: Platforms should protect user privacy while responding to regulatory data requests and establishing effective communication mechanisms.

4. **Exploration of Technical Solutions**: The industry is exploring technical solutions such as zero-knowledge proofs and on-chain identity protocols to meet compliance requirements while protecting privacy.

## FAQ

**Q1: What are the main differences between NFT domains and traditional domains?**

Traditional domains are managed centrally by ICANN with registration data stored in centralized databases. NFT domains operate on decentralized blockchain networks with ownership recorded through smart contracts and cryptographic methods, typically featuring immutability and censorship resistance (ENS Docs, 2023; ICANN, 2023).

**Q2: What is the legal effect of digital asset registration?**

In traditional legal systems, registration of real estate or corporate equity has clear legal effect. Whether NFT domain on-chain registration can receive equivalent recognition in all jurisdictions remains a topic requiring further research.

**Q3: How do platforms address regulatory requirements?**

Platforms like OpenSea incorporate compliance requirements through terms of service and implement fraud detection and content review. However, since actual asset transfers occur on-chain, platforms may face technical and legal complexities in assuming traditional financial intermediary responsibilities.
